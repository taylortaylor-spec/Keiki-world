About Keiki

Beautiful dinners.
Old trees.
Room for both.

I’m the kind of woman who loves a Michelin-starred dinner and will still stop on the way home to touch a tree. I like the care that goes into a beautiful meal, the small ceremony of getting dressed, the anticipation of seeing you across the table.

And I like standing beneath something older than either of us, resting my hand against its bark, wondering what it has lived through. There is room in my life for both. I hope there is in yours.

Between the pages

Finding rooms inside stories.

I’m Keiki, a writer and book curator whose curiosity has a habit of following her everywhere. It began with Wishbone and Charmed, with Archie comics tucked somewhere between the worlds of Anne Rice, Redwall, and Terry Goodkind.

I grew up wandering through stories, finding rooms inside them that I didn’t want to leave. Edgar Allan Poe gave my imagination somewhere dark to wander; William Faulkner, somewhere tangled. Robert Frost and Henry David Thoreau gave me reasons to look a little longer at the woods, while Shakespeare gave words to feelings I was still learning to name.

A curious mind

Something worth considering.

My shelves have grown with me. Literature leans against textbooks; philosophy keeps company with esoteric books. Carl Sagan and E. O. Wilson feed my wonder about the living world, while Richard Feynman reminds me how much pleasure there is in trying to understand something. Carl Jung draws me inward, toward the strange rooms of the mind.

I’m drawn, too, to the questions explored by J. B. and Louisa Rhine—the ones that keep a conversation going long after someone has suggested we call it a night.

Plato, Democritus, and Epictetus all have a place in that curiosity, though Pericles remains a particular favorite. My reading carries me toward Baruch Spinoza, Voltaire, and Jean-Jacques Rousseau, then somewhere else entirely: the life of Isabella of France, or a passage by Maya Angelou that asks me to sit still and feel what I’ve just read.

I like having my thoughts interrupted by something worth considering. I like people who can do that, too.

And yes, there is room among all those pages for Anakin and Ahsoka. Their story in The Clone Wars stayed with me. More recently, I’ve loved Sentenced to Be a Hero and Gachiakuta. Give them a watch; I’d enjoy hearing what you think.

Away from my books

Time to notice things.

Away from my books, I go looking for gardens and architecture that make me slow down. Smith Tower, the Seattle Central Library, an afternoon sightseeing in Tacoma, or a stay at Lake Quinault Lodge—all appeal to the part of me that wants to step inside a place and learn its story.

We could dress for the Seattle Opera, catch a Kraken game, or watch the Seawolves play. I love a rugby match. An evening at Willows Lodge after exploring the wineries would suit me beautifully, too. I enjoy having something to look forward to, especially when someone has put thought into choosing it.

Our time together

Quality over quantity.

I’m particular about the people I choose to spend time with. I want to arrive interested, with enough room in my mind to listen and enough warmth to make you feel welcome. Quality over quantity has always made sense to me: fewer meetings, more attention; time we can settle into without already reaching for what comes next.

I want you to leave feeling very good about the hours we shared, carrying some ease in your shoulders, a little brightness in your day, perhaps a thought you hadn’t considered before.

Something to remember

A little warmth to take with you.

I hope I make you curious the way trees swaying in the wind can make you look up and wonder what lies beyond them. I hope looking forward to me gives your morning that first-coffee feeling—the warmth, the lift, the sense that the day holds something good.

And when I see you, I want my smile to reach you before I do, as familiar and welcome as a warm hug. Something you can relax into. Something you remember on your way home.
